# Phase 4 — Final 3D Reconstruction

Current status (2026-08-02): T5 is an active implementation candidate with its
Human Gate pending. Product output is a physical-volume query package with
explicit support/unsupported masks and isolated serialized evaluation. No
scientific or clinical pass is implied by export or metric execution.

## 1. Objective

Convert the converged or budget-limited patient-specific representation into complete registered 3D MRI volumes, arbitrary physical slices, structural fields, and uncertainty maps.

The final output is not generated by reloading the hidden full volume. It is generated by evaluating the learned patient state at requested physical coordinates.

---

## 2. Inputs

```text
FinalPatientState
├── structural field bundle
├── refined anchors
├── structural Gaussian bank
├── volumetric appearance Gaussian bank
├── modality appearance slots
├── uncertainty and observability state
├── physical reference frame
└── trajectory and residual history
```

The state may have status `CONVERGED` or `INSUFFICIENTLY_OBSERVED`; output uncertainty and status must accompany every reconstruction.

---

## 3. Reconstruction coordinate system

Choose a target physical grid:

```text
TargetGrid
├── origin_xyz
├── direction matrix
├── spacing_xyz
├── shape_xyz
└── requested modalities
```

The grid may match a reference MRI volume, use an isotropic research grid, or define arbitrary slice planes.

Every output voxel center is converted to a physical coordinate \(\mathbf x\).

---

## 4. Structural-field evaluation

For each point \(\mathbf x\), evaluate relevant local fields and blend them:

\[
F(\mathbf x)=
\frac{\sum_i w_i(\mathbf x)f_i(\mathbf x)}
{\sum_iw_i(\mathbf x)+\epsilon}.
\]

Possible outputs:

- signed distance or structural level-set value;
- local gradient and normal;
- zero-level surfaces;
- support coverage;
- structural uncertainty.

If the field is not demonstrably a true SDF, documentation and evaluation must call it a structural level-set field rather than overclaiming signed-distance properties.

---

## 5. Modality appearance reconstruction

For modality \(m\), each Gaussian contributes an appearance value or compact code.

The default low-complexity renderer is normalized additive composition:

\[
\hat V^m(\mathbf x)=
\frac{
\sum_i w_i(\mathbf x)c_{i,m}
}{
\sum_iw_i(\mathbf x)+\epsilon
}.
\]

This avoids a large global decoder. A tiny residual appearance correction may be studied separately, but it must justify its accuracy–FLOP trade-off.

---

## 6. Structural versus volumetric contributions

### Structural Gaussians

Thin, surface-aligned primitives contribute strongly near interfaces and preserve high-frequency geometry.

### Volumetric Gaussians

Larger 3D primitives represent interior tissue intensity and low-frequency appearance.

A reconstruction point may receive both contributions:

\[
\hat V^m(\mathbf x)=
\operatorname{Fuse}
(
\hat V_{surface}^m(\mathbf x),
\hat V_{volume}^m(\mathbf x),
F(\mathbf x)
).
\]

The simplest fusion is a normalized joint sum. More elaborate gates are optional research variants.

---

## 7. Slice reconstruction

To reconstruct a physical slice plane:

```text
plane origin, row vector, column vector, spacing
→ generate physical coordinate for each output pixel
→ evaluate structural and Gaussian fields
→ compose requested modality intensity
→ return 2D image and uncertainty map
```

This renderer treats MRI slices as physical planar cross-sections, not perspective camera views.

---

## 8. Full-volume reconstruction

For each target modality:

1. partition target grid into memory-safe chunks;
2. query local anchor neighborhoods;
3. evaluate only Gaussians whose support intersects the chunk;
4. blend local structural and appearance values;
5. write reconstructed intensity and uncertainty;
6. preserve original physical metadata in the exported volume.

The result should support NIfTI or another medical-volume format without losing affine information.

---

## 9. Uncertainty reconstruction

A pointwise uncertainty estimate may combine:

\[
U(\mathbf x)=
\lambda_dU_{distance}
+\lambda_cU_{coverage}
+\lambda_mU_{missing-modality}
+\lambda_gU_{Gaussian-disagreement}
+\lambda_rU_{residual}
+\lambda_pU_{predictive}.
\]

The system should export both:

- a total uncertainty volume;
- decomposed uncertainty channels when possible.

This allows evaluation of whether uncertainty reflects sparse observation, model disagreement, or modality absence.

## 11. Evaluation protocol

### Intensity fidelity

- MAE;
- NMSE;
- PSNR;
- SSIM / MS-SSIM;
- normalized cross-correlation.

### Structural fidelity

- gradient error;
- edge reconstruction error;
- frequency-band error;
- surface distance for derived structures when labels are available;
- local contrast preservation.

### Region-sensitive evaluation

Even when segmentation is not the primary task, report reconstruction in clinically relevant or high-frequency regions when labels are available only for evaluation:

- lesion-region PSNR/SSIM/MAE;
- boundary-band reconstruction error;
- small-structure recall through a frozen downstream model;
- downstream segmentation fidelity on reconstructed volumes.

### Budgeted evaluation

Report quality as a function of queried slices:

\[
Q(B),
\qquad
\operatorname{AUC}_B,
\qquad
B_{target}.
\]

The key claim is not only final quality, but quality gained per additional observation and per unit compute.

---

## 12. Baselines

Required comparisons should include:

- interpolation from sparse slices;
- fixed uniform slice selection;
- random slice selection;
- single-sequence reconstruction;
- dense voxel or convolutional reconstruction model under comparable observation budget;
- free Gaussian representation;
- SDF-initialized but unconstrained Gaussians;
- persistent SDF-constrained Gaussians;
- single-wave versus balanced multi-wave routing;
- direct sparse encoder variants at matched inference FLOPs.

---

## 13. Reconstruction integrity checks

Before exporting:

- verify affine and orientation consistency;
- verify output intensity normalization and inverse normalization;
- report unsupported voxels rather than silently filling with certainty;
- check denominator stability in Gaussian blending;
- verify queried slices can be re-rendered consistently;
- store reconstruction status and observation budget;
- preserve modality identity;
- ensure hidden evaluation volumes were never loaded into inference state.

---

## 14. Scientific interpretation

A visually smooth volume is not sufficient evidence of successful reconstruction. The method must demonstrate that:

- observed details are preserved;
- hidden structures are reconstructed rather than erased by population priors;
- uncertainty rises where evidence is insufficient;
- active trajectory improves quality–budget curves;
- sparse local representation provides a measurable compute or memory advantage;
- improvements are not solely caused by a larger encoder or decoder.
